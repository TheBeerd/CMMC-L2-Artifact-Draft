Risk Matrix — General FCI / PII & General CUI

General FCI / PII Matrix

Risk LevelDescriptionExamples
1 – LowNo sensitive information is exposed. Minimal or no impact if accessed.Publicly available data, general system logs, anonymized metrics.
2 – Low-ModerateLimited exposure to non-critical internal information. Low impact.Internal documentation, non-sensitive emails, basic user preferences.
3 – ModerateExposure to moderately sensitive data. Potential for reputational or operational impact.FCI, Employee IDs, internal project details, limited access credentials.
4 – HighExposure to sensitive or regulated data. Significant impact if compromised.CUI, PII (e.g., names, addresses), internal financials, restricted access systems.
5 – CriticalExposure to highly sensitive or confidential data. Severe legal, financial, or reputational consequences.PHI, SSNs, passwords, encryption keys, legal documents, customer data.

General CUI Risk Matrix

Risk LevelDescriptionExamples
1 – LowMinimal impact if CUI is accessed. Access is tightly controlled and monitored.Access by authorized personnel in a secure, isolated environment.
2 – Low-ModerateLimited impact. CUI is non-sensitive or already publicly available in part.Access to sanitized CUI for training or testing purposes.
3 – ModerateModerate impact. CUI is sensitive but not mission-critical.Internal access by staff with need-to-know, but without multi-factor authentication.
4 – HighHigh impact. CUI is critical to operations or compliance.Access by third parties or contractors without full vetting or encryption in transit.
5 – CriticalSevere impact. Unauthorized access could result in legal, financial, or national security consequences.Breach of CUI involving export-controlled data or PII/PHI without proper safeguards.
